A-00 · A method & open toolset for building software together

Software, drawn before it is built.

épure is a working method and an open toolset for building software together — domain owners, developers, AI assistants — in organizations that cannot afford surprises: public services, regulated industries, large teams. The drawing comes first: contracts written in the domain's own words, signed by the people who own the need, honored by the machines that build. What was drawn is what runs — and the work at the table stays convivial.

§ 01Structure

The master lines

Four rules carry everything. They make an application legible to the next reviewer — whether that reviewer is a colleague, an auditor, or a machine.

P-1

Contracts before code

Every behavior starts as a *.feature file: plain-language scenarios that business reviews and signs before the AI writes a line of code. The same file is executable — the contract and the test suite are one artifact, so they can never drift apart.

P-2

Four bounded floors

Features hold the business — state, derived values and actions carved into one object, the logic pure functions. Repos persist, one object per saved type. Services are named and deliberately few. Views project state, with no logic of their own. Each floor is testable alone — and replaceable alone.

P-3

Reactive by construction

Application state flows to the screen through tilia. Data is loaded ahead of navigation and refreshed in the background — no loading spinner on every page.

P-4

Offline, online — both

Local-first reads answer instantly; a durable outbox replays writes when the network returns. The same application works in the field, in the basement, and in the audit room.

In a repository, the method is one page: CONTRIBUTING.md — the working agreement everyone reads on arrival. Humans find it by habit; agents are sent by a one-line AGENTS.md that points them to it. One table, one set of promises.

§ 02Governance

The development window

AI-assisted development stays safe when it is bounded. All change happens inside a window: it opens on a validated need and closes on an audited result. Between windows, the system is stable — and nobody “quickly patches” anything.

01

The need

Business states a clear need — in its own words, with the outcome it expects and the value attached to it. No window opens without one.

Business
02 The window opens Bounded scope · named people · end date
03

Pair prompting

A domain expert and a method expert sit together and draft the change as Gherkin *.feature files — plain-language scenarios anyone in the room can read. This is the épure.

Expert × Expert
04

The contract is signed

The feature files are reviewed and validated by the people who own the need. From here on they are the contract the AI must honor — and, run by @epure/vitest, the suite that enforces it.

Business signs
05

Build & iterate

The AI implements against the signed scenarios; the contracts anchor every prompt and keep hallucination out of the codebase. A senior reviews on a fixed cadence — every few hours or days, never only at the end.

AI builds · senior reviews
06

Validation & testing

Every scenario runs green, and the result is demonstrated to the people who signed the need. What was drawn is what was built — provably.

Suite + business
07

Security audit

Before anything ships, an audit walks the diff, the dependencies and the data paths. The window's bounded scope is exactly what makes this audit tractable.

Auditor
08 The window closes Stable — maintenance continues, the next need reopens
§ 03Scope

What the method does not decide

épure governs how an application changes safely during its life. The questions around that life belong to the organization, and no method makes them disappear — this one included.

  • Birth Whether the artifact should exist at all: build or buy, prototype or product.
  • Succession Who owns it when its author moves on, and what happens when that name goes stale.
  • Death When it is retired, and what “unmaintained” triggers.

These are answered before the first window opens.

§ 04Open source

The toolset

Small tools, each doing one job. Together they make the method practical: reactive state, predictable remote data, and contracts that run.

SHEET A-01 Foundation

tilia

tilia /ti·li·a/ n., Latin — the linden. The carver's wood: even-grained, cuts clean, holds detail.

Reactive state that stays in the domain's shape: plain objects and pure functions, observed directly by views — no stores, no dispatch.

  • domain objects, observed as-is
  • computed values, no boilerplate
  • TypeScript & ReScript

SHEET A-02 Data

@tilia/query

workshop name sève /sɛv/ n., f. — the sap. Moves between root and crown; held through winter, flowing again at thaw.

Query state on top of tilia: a shared object cache, query views, background staleness refresh, and a durable write outbox that survives restarts — offline-friendly by design.

  • local + remote two-tier reads
  • optimistic writes, replayed on reconnect
  • live updates merged in place

SHEET A-03 Proof

@epure/vitest

workshop name compas /kɔ̃·pa/ n., m. — the divider. Steps the drawing off against the work.

Contracts that run: every scenario file — Gherkin or YAML — is also a Vitest suite, so what business signed is exactly what the tests check.

  • translated in memory, no generated files
  • steps are ordinary functions
  • runs in CI and in watch mode
